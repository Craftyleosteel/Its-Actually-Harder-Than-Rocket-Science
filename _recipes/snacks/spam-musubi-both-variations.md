## Ingredients

- 1 can of SPAM of your desired flavor
- 2 cups of sushi rice (can vary depending on how much rice you like)
- 3 sheets of Nori 
- Furikake
- Soy Sauce
- Mirin
- Oyster Sauce 
- Rice Vinegar 
- 1 clove of garlic
- Honey

## Steps

1. First wash your rice well until the water runs clear. Then place in your rice cooker and let it cook for however long it needs
2. Next, slice your SPAM the long way. Be sure to save your SPAM container--this will be useful later! 
Depending on the texture you prefer, you can slice you SPAM however thick you;d like. I have had a good time with this both thick or crispy. For my preference I cut it about half the height of my finger nails to get the best of both worlds. 
3. Now with yout pan on medium low heat, place your slices of SPAM down being sure to not over crowd your pan. 
4. While the first side begins to sear, roughly mince your garlic and mix up your sauce. 
5. Once your sauce is mixed flip your spam and keep letting your spam get cooked until you have your ideal sear. 
6. Once you are basically done add your sauce to the pan and let it cook down until the sauce is nice and reduced. 
7. Once your sauce is nice and thick, slice your nori sheets into fat strips to wrap your musubi. 
8. Using your SPAM containter from before, first put some cling wrap or whatever you have around to get your musubi out of the container. Then a bit of rice at the bottom (I usually like a lot of rice) then sprinkle a bit of furikake on top. 
9. Put your SPAM on top of your now perfectly sized rice ball and wrap with your nori 

## Notes

This recipe can be adapted in so many ways, I have never followed a recipe for making the glaze for the SPAM. I always measure that shit with my heart rather than follow precise measurements. I also usually just make the glaze from a soy base and what ever else is in my pantry at the time. I like to add honey and rice wine vinegar to mine because it adds and extra little kick that I enjoy. 

This recipe keeps well throughout a day or two, but is best enjoyed day of. I would always recommend bringing friends over and cooking these up for a sports game or for really any event where people can eat meat.
